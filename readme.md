# [SAR Imaging] UAV Synthetic Aperture Radar Imaging Based on Time-Domain Back Projection [with MATLAB Code]

The radar system mounted on the drone captures echo signals from the environment, and the project aims to generate detailed images of the scanning area by processing these echoes.
Time-domain Back Projection (TDBP) Algorithm utilizes multiple radar pulses to generate images from radar data. The algorithm processes the echoes of each pulse and obtains the coherent image of the environment by integrating these echoes over different time samples.
1) Distance resolution assessment This section focuses on evaluating the distance resolution of radar systems, including both known bandwidth and unknown bandwidth scenarios. The resolution of distance refers to the ability of radar to distinguish two targets that are close in distance. We calculate this resolution using a two-step method.
Unknown Bandwidth: Under unknown bandwidth conditions, we aim to estimate the distance resolution. The first step is to calculate the sampling frequency based on the provided data; once the sampling frequency is obtained, perform a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) on each radar pulse to convert it into the frequency domain; from this, calculate the average frequency and use this frequency to estimate the bandwidth. 6) Impact of Tracking Errors
This section explores the impact of tracking errors (caused by inaccuracies in the UAV navigation system) on SAR image quality. To simulate these errors, white noise was added to the trajectory data (X, Y, Z coordinates), and different standard deviations (λ/10, λ/6, λ/4, and λ/2) were set to represent varying degrees of navigation inaccuracy.
Low Noise Level When the noise is small (λ/10), the SAR image remains largely clear with only minor distortions.
(Figure 7: SAR image corresponding to trajectory noise with a standard deviation of λ/10)
High Noise Level As the noise increases (λ/6, λ/4, λ/2), the image gradually becomes blurry and distorted, with details progressively lost.
(Figure 8: SAR image corresponding to trajectory noise with a standard deviation of λ/6)
(Figure 9: SAR image corresponding to trajectory noise with a standard deviation of λ/4)
